Hearing Accessibility Guide

Videos, lessons, and alerts often use sound for important information, but not everyone can hear audio, and some people browse with sound off. Hearing accessibility offers other ways to follow along.

Add accurate captions

Captions show spoken words and meaningful sounds in videos so viewers can follow without audio.

Provide transcripts

A transcript is a written version of audio content that people can read at their own pace.

Use visual notifications

Important alerts should appear on screen instead of being communicated through sound alone.

Offer accessible media controls

Let users pause, replay, and control media whenever possible.

Make instructions available in text

Do not rely only on spoken directions. Written instructions help more people understand the next step.

Everyday example

An online lesson with accurate captions lets students follow the discussion even when they cannot hear the video.

Key takeaway

Hearing accessibility makes information easier to follow by offering visual and written alternatives to sound.
